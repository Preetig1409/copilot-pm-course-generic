The 10 Testing Lenses

Every module is evaluated against 10 lenses. Two are common (applied by all teams with equal rigor). Eight are team-owned (2 per team — these teams go deeper and are the authoritative voice).

Common Lenses (all teams, equal depth)

L1: Content Accuracy

"Is what this module states factually correct?"

CheckWhat to look for
Technical correctnessAre M365 Copilot features, UI elements, menu paths, and shortcuts described accurately?
Version currencyDo references match current Copilot capabilities, or do they describe deprecated/non-existent features?
Data accuracyAre metrics, statistics, and company context internally consistent across modules?
Link & reference integrityDo cross-references to other modules, external resources, and sample files resolve correctly?
Terminology consistencyAre terms (AKX, ADH, persona names, feature names) used consistently throughout?

Reporting standard: Flag with exact quote and correction. Severity is always Major or Critical for factual errors.

L2: Learner Clarity

"If I were an enterprise PM picking this up for the first time, could I follow every step without guessing?"

CheckWhat to look for
Instruction precisionCan you complete each step exactly as written? Or did you have to interpret, infer, or Google?
Assumed knowledgeDoes the module assume skills or context it hasn't taught and doesn't reference?
Ambiguous languageAre there phrases like "set up your environment" without specifying what that means?
Visual orientationWhen UI is referenced, is it clear where to look? (Screenshots, nav paths, or precise descriptions)
Jargon loadIs enterprise/PM/Copilot jargon introduced before it's used?

Reporting standard: Quote the confusing passage, explain what tripped you up, suggest clearer wording.

Team-Owned Lenses

L3: Prompt Effectiveness (Team 1)

"When I copy this prompt into Copilot and run it, does it actually produce something useful?"

CheckWhat to look for
Functional promptsCopy every prompt from the module into Copilot. Does it run? Does it produce output matching what the module promises?
Output qualityIs Copilot's response genuinely useful for the stated PM task, or generic filler?
Prompt robustnessDoes the prompt work with slight variations (different product names, different data), or is it brittle?
Prompt rationaleDoes the module explain why the prompt is structured that way? (Teaching to fish, not just giving fish)
Edge casesWhat happens with enterprise-specific constraints (long documents, multiple stakeholders, ambiguous data)?

Reporting standard: Include the prompt used, Copilot's actual output (screenshot or paste), and what output was expected.

L4: Exercise Quality (Team 1)

"Are the hands-on activities well-designed, completable, and genuinely teaching the skill?"

CheckWhat to look for
CompletabilityCan you finish the exercise with the materials provided? Are sample files sufficient?
ScaffoldingDoes the exercise build progressively, or does it dump complexity at once?
Success criteriaIs it clear what "done well" looks like? Can the learner self-assess?
Time realismDoes the exercise fit within the module's stated time? (Time yourself.)
Learning alignmentDoes the exercise practice the skill the module claims to teach, or something adjacent?

Reporting standard: Note which exercise, time taken vs. stated, and what was confusing or missing.

L5: Instructional Design (Team 2)

"Does this module teach effectively? Would I recommend this design to a client building training?"

CheckWhat to look for
Learning objectivesAre objectives stated at the top? Are they measurable? Does the content actually deliver them?
Cognitive loadIs information density appropriate? Are there natural pause points, or is it a wall of text?
Retention hooksAre there summaries, key takeaways, "try this" moments, or reflection prompts?
Multi-modal learningIs there a mix of reading, doing, and reviewing — or is it all one mode?
Assessment opportunityCan the learner check their own understanding before moving on?

Reporting standard: Reference specific learning objectives and whether the content delivers on each.

L6: Progressive Flow (Team 2)

"Does the course build logically from module to module? Does difficulty ramp naturally?"

CheckWhat to look for
Cross-module sequencingDoes each module build on what the previous ones taught? Are there jarring jumps?
Prerequisite handlingWhen a module requires knowledge from an earlier one, is this stated or linked?
Difficulty progressionDoes Level 1 to Level 2 to Level 3 feel like a genuine progression, or are levels arbitrary?
Repetition vs. reinforcementIs repeated content intentional reinforcement, or accidental redundancy?
Completion arcDoes the full course feel like a journey with a satisfying endpoint, or does it just stop?

Reporting standard: Map the dependency/flow issues you find across modules — this lens is inherently cross-module.

L7: Enterprise Realism (Team 3)

"Does this feel like a real enterprise environment, or a textbook simulation?"

CheckWhat to look for
Company context authenticityDoes the Accenture scenario (700K employees, AKX, ADH) feel like working at a large consultancy?
Persona believabilityAre Priya (Partner), Marcus (Consultant), and Elena (Delivery Manager) realistic? Do their concerns ring true?
Enterprise complexityDoes the course acknowledge real enterprise friction — governance, politics, legacy systems, stakeholder management?
Sample data realismDo meeting notes, interview transcripts, and data files feel like real enterprise artifacts or sanitized examples?
Scale considerationsDo solutions work at enterprise scale (thousands of users, multiple BUs, global teams)?

Reporting standard: Draw on your own client engagement experience. Flag where the simulation breaks compared to reality.

L8: Real-World Applicability (Team 3)

"Could a PM take what they learned here and use it at their desk tomorrow?"

CheckWhat to look for
Transferable skillsAre techniques taught in a way that generalizes beyond the AKX/ADH example?
Immediate usefulnessAfter completing a module, could you use that skill in your current client engagement this week?
Template extractabilityCan prompts, frameworks, and templates be copied and adapted for real projects?
Environment dependencyDoes the skill work only in the specific setup described, or across typical enterprise M365 environments?
Organizational readinessDoes the course address real adoption barriers (manager buy-in, data sensitivity, IT policies)?

Reporting standard: For each module, rate "I could use this tomorrow" on a 1-5 scale, with explanation.

L9: Navigation & UX (Team 4)

"Is the course site itself easy to use, or does the delivery platform get in the way of learning?"

CheckWhat to look for
Site navigationCan you find any module in under 3 clicks? Is the course structure obvious?
Cross-module linkingWhen a module references another, is there a working link?
Mobile experienceDoes the site work on a tablet/phone? (Enterprise PMs often review content on the go.)
Rendering qualityDo markdown tables, code blocks, and formatting render correctly?
Progress trackingDoes the progress system work? Does it persist across sessions? Is it motivating?
PerformanceAre load times acceptable? Any broken assets, missing images, or console errors?

Reporting standard: Include browser/device info, screenshot of the issue, and steps to reproduce.

L10: Gaps & Completeness (Team 4)

"What question would a learner have that this course doesn't answer?"

CheckWhat to look for
Missing topicsAre there Copilot capabilities or PM workflows that should be covered but aren't?
Unanswered questionsAt any point, did you think "but what about...?" and the course never addressed it?
Audience blind spotsDoes the course assume a specific PM profile (e.g., technical PM) and miss others (e.g., business PM, design PM)?
Copilot limitationsDoes the course acknowledge where Copilot falls short, or does it only show the happy path?
Post-course pathAfter completing all 15 modules, does the learner know what to do next?

Reporting standard: Frame each gap as "A learner would expect to find [X] but the course doesn't cover it because [Y is missing]."

How "Focus More, Not Only" Works

Every tester applies all 10 lenses while going through each module. The difference:

Lens typeWhat you doFeedback expectation
Common lenses (L1, L2)Full attention, equal across all teamsFlag every issue you find
Your team's owned lensesDeep, deliberate focus — this is your primary jobDetailed findings with evidence; you are the authority
Other teams' owned lensesNotice in passing while going through the moduleFlag only obvious/glaring issues; don't go hunting

This means each module gets:

  • 4x coverage on common lenses (all teams)
  • 1x deep coverage on each owned lens pair (the assigned team)
  • 3x light coverage on each owned lens pair (the other teams, catching obvious things)

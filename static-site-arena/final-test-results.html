Five Key Findings

1. Keyboard shortcuts are wrong across the board

All 4 teams independently found that Alt+Spacebar, Alt+I, and Alt+L do not work as documented. This is the single most-reported issue (15+ findings). Learners will lose trust in the first 5 minutes.

2. Time-savings numbers contradict each other

The course claims “10–20 hours/week,” then “5–8 hours,” then “14+ hours.” Three different numbers on the same topic. Learners notice this immediately.

3. PM vs PO/BA role confusion runs through every module

Course title says “Product Managers.” Content says “Senior Product Owner.” Neither PO nor BA is consistently addressed. Three teams flagged this. The audience needs a single, clear definition.

4. No enterprise data safety warnings anywhere

The course teaches uploading files to Copilot but never warns about sensitive data, confidentiality labels, or enterprise AI policies. Two teams flagged this as a real-world risk.

5. File upload limit claim is factually incorrect

Course states “Copilot can analyze up to 5 files at once.” Three teams verified it accepts more than 5. This is a testable, verifiable error.

Overall Assessment

The course structure, exercise design, and enterprise context are genuinely strong. Testers praised the prompts as practical and reusable. The problems are concentrated in factual accuracy (wrong shortcuts, wrong numbers, outdated model names) and audience clarity (PM vs PO/BA). These are high-impact but low-effort fixes. The course does not need a redesign. It needs a factual accuracy pass and a single audience decision.

What Changed Since Interim

Coverage expanded from front-loaded (mostly modules 1.1–1.3) to full 15/15 modules. Teams completed all owned-lens testing. Peer review reached 48% (up from near-zero). Advanced modules (Level 2–3) now have real feedback. The same core issues from interim (shortcuts, role confusion, time-savings numbers) persist — confirming they are genuine problems, not one-off observations.

Prioritization Criteria

Every finding is prioritized by one question: “Does this make the course wrong, confusing, or incomplete for a PO/BA learner?”

P0 — Fix Now Factually incorrect. Learner follows the instruction and it does not work. Wrong shortcuts, wrong numbers, wrong model names. Erodes trust immediately.
P1 — Fix Before Launch Ambiguous or misleading. Learner is confused about who the course is for, what to expect, or how to interpret conflicting information. Blocks learning.
P2 — Fix Soon Missing but important. No enterprise safety warnings, no error handling guidance, no prerequisites. Learner can proceed but may hit real-world problems.
P3 — Backlog Enhancement. Industry-specific exercises, better visual examples, module restructuring. Good ideas but not blocking correctness or clarity.

Cross-Cutting Themes

10 patterns confirmed across multiple teams. Sorted by priority.

Keyboard shortcuts are wrong or inconsistent P0

Modules: 1.1, 1.2, 1.7, 2.2  |  Teams: All 4  |  15 findings

Alt+Spacebar does not open Copilot. Alt+I does not work in Excel or PowerPoint. Alt+L does not work. Shortcuts vary by app version, OS, and tenant configuration. The course presents these as guaranteed.

Fix: Replace specific shortcuts with conditional instructions: “If enabled in your version, press Alt+I. Otherwise, click the Copilot icon in the toolbar.” Add a disclaimer that shortcuts vary by environment.

PM vs PO/BA role ambiguity P0

Modules: 1.1–1.5  |  Teams: Team 2, 3, 4  |  11 findings

Course title says “Product Managers.” Modules say “Senior Product Owner.” Persona prompts are PM-only. Course is also meant for BAs per the objective, but BA is rarely mentioned.

Fix: Make one decision: either rename to “PO/BA” throughout, or keep “PM” and add a note that the skills apply equally to POs and BAs. Apply consistently across all 15 modules.

File upload limit claim is incorrect P0

Modules: 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.6, 3.1  |  Teams: Team 1, 2, 4  |  8 findings

Course states “Copilot can analyze up to 5 files at once.” Testers uploaded 8 files successfully. The distinction between uploading and analyzing is unclear.

Fix: Verify the actual current limit. Update all references. Distinguish between upload limit and analysis limit if they differ.

Time-savings numbers contradict each other P1

Modules: 1.1, 2.3  |  Teams: Team 2, 4  |  4 findings

Three different claims: “10–20 hours/week,” “5–8 hours,” and “14+ hours.” No explanation for the range difference.

Fix: Pick one defensible number range. If different levels of savings exist, explain the tiers explicitly (e.g., “5–8 hours for beginners, 10–20 for power users”).

Enterprise data sensitivity warnings missing P1

Modules: 1.2, 1.6, 1.7, 2.2, 2.3  |  Teams: Team 3, 4  |  6 findings

The course teaches uploading documents and using memory features but never warns about confidential data, sensitivity labels, or enterprise AI policies. Team 3 found that sensitivity-labeled files block Copilot entirely.

Fix: Add a boxed warning in Module 1.2 (first file upload). Repeat in modules that handle sensitive data (1.6, 2.2, 2.3). Include: “Do not upload confidential or sensitivity-labeled documents unless your organization permits it.”

Microsoft Designer access assumed but not available to all P1

Modules: 1.1, 3.1, 3.2  |  Teams: Team 2, 3, 4  |  6 findings

Modules 3.1 and 3.2 assume Designer is available. Many enterprise tenants do not have it enabled. No fallback path is provided.

Fix: Add a prerequisite check. Provide an “If Designer is not available” fallback path using Copilot Chat image generation only.

No assessments or success criteria for exercises P2

Modules: 1.4, 3.1, All  |  Teams: Team 2, 3  |  3 findings

Exercises ask learners to generate outputs but never define what “good” looks like. No sample outputs, no checklists, no assessments at any level.

Fix: Add a simple success checklist to each exercise. For visual exercises, add a sample output image or description.

“Level 1 Complete” congratulations appears too early P2

Modules: 1.7, 1.9  |  Teams: Team 2  |  2 findings

Module 1.7 shows “Congratulations! Level 1 Complete!” but modules 1.8–1.10 are still in Level 1. Same issue at module 1.9.

Fix: Move the completion message to module 1.10, or restructure levels so 1.7 is actually the end of a level.

Module length causes cognitive overload P3

Modules: 2.1, 2.3, 3.2  |  Teams: Team 2, 4  |  6 findings

Modules 2.1 and 2.3 are very long vertical pages. Module 2.3 is described as “a 3–4 hour workshop compressed into 35 minutes.” Learners skim rather than engage.

Fix: Consider breaking 2.3 into sub-modules. Add collapsible sections and “pause and try” moments to long modules.

Industry-specific exercises requested P3

Modules: 1.5, 1.6  |  Teams: Team 3  |  2 findings

Team 3 suggested adding industry-specific exercises (Pharma, Finance, Manufacturing) for broader applicability.

Fix: Consider adding optional industry variants as stretch exercises. Not required for initial launch.
